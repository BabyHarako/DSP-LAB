01 / AUDIO

Amplitude Scaling

Plays DFT.wav as-is, then normalizes it by dividing every sample by the signal's own maximum value, then attenuates the normalized signal to 20% amplitude. Listen for the loudness change between all three.

ExpChap1_1.m

Live demo

y = y / max(max(y)) divides by the raw maximum (not the absolute maximum) — if the signal leans more negative than positive, normalizing this way can push some samples past ±1 and clip. That's the "why?" the original comment is pointing at.

Sampling Frequency Scaling

    
Same samples, played back as if the sample rate were different. Playing at half the original rate stretches the signal and drops the pitch by an octave; playing at double the rate compresses it and raises the pitch by an octave.

05 / AUDIO

    
Absolute Value

    
Rectifies the signal by taking abs(y) — every negative sample is folded up to positive, so the waveform below the zero line mirrors above it. Doubles the perceived frequency content since each cycle now produces two humps.

Delay & Add (Echo)
